RFoldSearch User Guide

RFoldSearch is a web server for the rapid retrieval of RNA tertiary structures. It encodes the local three-dimensional geometry at each nucleotide as a discrete symbol from a learned structural alphabet (3Dr), recasting structure comparison as the alignment of structural-token sequences. This representation enables indexed, large-scale searches against databases of experimentally determined and computationally predicted RNA structures.

This guide describes the preparation and submission of query structures, the configuration of searches, the interpretation of reported metrics, and the inspection of structural alignments. Methodological details are provided in the RFoldSearch manuscript.

1. Query submission

1.1 Input formats

Queries are submitted on the search page by one of three routes:

  • File upload: an RNA structure in PDB or mmCIF format.
  • Direct input: PDB- or mmCIF-formatted coordinate text pasted into the input field.
  • Example query: a pre-loaded RNA structure provided for demonstration (Load example).

1.2 Input requirements

CriterionRequirement
Molecule typeThe file must contain RNA atomic coordinates.
Nucleotide validityA nucleotide is considered valid only if its C3′, C4′ and O4′ atoms are present; other nucleotides are skipped.
Minimum lengthThe query must contain at least 4 valid nucleotides.
Multi-chain inputWhere multiple RNA chains are present, only the first RNA chain is used.
File sizeUp to the size shown under the input box, for uploaded and pasted structures alike.

2. Target databases

RFoldSearch currently supports searches against three structure collections. One or more databases may be selected for a given query.

DatabaseContent
PDB-RNAExperimentally determined RNA structures deposited in the Protein Data Bank
RNAsolo 2.0A non-redundant set of experimentally determined RNA structures
NuFoldA large-scale collection of predicted RNA structures. The models carry no sequence, so 3Dr identity is reported in place of sequence identity.

3. Search modes

Two search modes are available, representing a trade-off between computational cost and ranking accuracy.

ModeProcedure
RFoldSearch (default)Structural-token retrieval followed by ranking with a combined token-alignment and geometric score
RFoldSearch-TMStructural-token candidate retrieval followed by coordinate-based reranking with US-align

The default RFoldSearch mode is recommended for most applications. Upon submission, candidate structures are identified through the indexed structural-token search pipeline and returned as a ranked list for each selected database. By default, up to 50 hits are reported per database.

4. Search parameters

Additional parameters are accessible through the Settings panel.

ParameterDefinitionDefault
Hits shownMaximum number of hits reported per database50
Candidate depthNumber of candidates retained for downstream alignment and ranking200
Min. diagonal k-mersMinimum number of compatible diagonal k-mer seeds required for a target to be retained as a candidate2

The default values represent a practical balance between computational efficiency and retrieval sensitivity. Increasing the candidate depth may recover additional, more distantly related structures at greater computational cost. Modifying the seed threshold alters the stringency of candidate selection: higher thresholds increase selectivity, whereas lower thresholds increase sensitivity at the expense of a larger candidate pool.

5. Interpretation of search results

5.1 Reported metrics

Each search returns a ranked table of structural matches. Click a column heading to sort by it, and a row to open the hit.

FieldDefinition
TargetIdentifier of the matched RNA structure: the PDB entry with its chain, or the RNAcentral id of a predicted model (linked to the entry page)
RNA / OrganismRNA description, source organism, and available annotations (for predicted models, the Rfam family when one is assigned)
pLDDTPredicted local structural confidence (reported for predicted structures where available)
Seq identityNucleotide sequence identity over aligned positions. Databases without sequences report 3Dr identity instead: the fraction of aligned pairs with the same structural token.
RMSD (Å)Root-mean-square deviation of aligned nucleotides after optimal superposition
Structure scoreComposite score integrating token-alignment and geometric similarity (Section 5.2)
TM-scoreLength-normalised measure of global structural similarity (Section 5.3)
E-valueStatistical significance of the structural-token alignment score (Section 5.5)
Query coverageFraction of query nucleotides included in the alignment
Target coverageFraction of target nucleotides included in the alignment

5.2 Structure score

In the default mode, hits are ranked by a structure score that combines structural-token alignment similarity with geometric agreement:

Rstr = SSW × aTM

where SSW denotes the Smith–Waterman alignment score computed over structural-token sequences, and aTM denotes the TM-score of the token alignment after structural superposition (the TM-score column in the default mode). Higher values of Rstr indicate stronger matches under the RFoldSearch ranking function.

5.3 TM-score

The TM-score quantifies global three-dimensional structural similarity on a scale from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating closer structural correspondence. Because it is normalised by length, it is less sensitive to local deviations than RMSD. RFoldSearch reports the mean of the query- and target-normalised scores: in the default mode for the token alignment, and in RFoldSearch-TM mode the US-align TM-score of the two chains. The hit view shows the same value as the table.

5.4 RMSD

The RMSD quantifies the mean geometric deviation, in ångströms, between aligned nucleotides after optimal superposition. As RMSD is computed only over aligned positions, it should be interpreted in conjunction with alignment coverage: a low RMSD obtained over a short alignment does not by itself indicate extensive structural similarity.

5.5 E-value

The E-value represents the expected number of unrelated database entries that would attain a structural-token alignment score equal to or greater than the observed score under the statistical background model. Lower E-values denote greater statistical significance. Databases that have not yet been calibrated report no E-value.

5.6 Alignment coverage

Query and target coverage together characterise the extent of the detected similarity:

  • High query and high target coverage: the alignment encompasses a substantial proportion of both structures, consistent with global structural similarity.
  • High query coverage, low target coverage: the query corresponds to a structural region within a larger target RNA, for example a domain or subdomain.
  • Low query and target coverage: the similarity is likely confined to a local structural motif.

Coverage should therefore be considered explicitly when distinguishing local motif-level matches from global structural correspondence.

6. Inspection of structural alignments

Selecting a hit opens a detailed view comprising the structural-token alignment and an interactive three-dimensional superposition.

6.1 Structural-token alignment

The alignment displays corresponding nucleotide positions together with their assigned 3Dr structural states. The relationship between each pair of aligned tokens is annotated as follows:

SymbolInterpretation
|Identical structural tokens
+Favourable structural-token substitution
.Other aligned token pair

These annotations describe similarity in local structural state and are independent of nucleotide sequence identity. Positions count valid nucleotides only; databases without sequences show the target residues as ·.

6.2 Three-dimensional superposition

The structure viewer displays the query and target after superposition, using the following colour scheme:

  • Orange: query RNA
  • Blue: target RNA
  • Pale shading: unaligned regions

Structures may be rotated and zoomed to assess structural correspondence. Selecting a position in either the alignment or the viewer highlights the corresponding nucleotides in both representations.

7. Case study: identification of structurally related RNAs

The following workflow illustrates the use of RFoldSearch to identify candidate structural relatives of an RNA of unknown function.

  1. Query submission. Upload the query structure in PDB or mmCIF format.
  2. Database selection. Select RNAsolo 2.0 to search a non-redundant set of experimentally determined structures.
  3. Mode selection. Use the default RFoldSearch mode.
  4. Initial screening. Execute the search and examine the highest-ranking hits.
  5. Metric assessment. Evaluate TM-score, RMSD, query and target coverage, and functional annotations for each candidate.
  6. Alignment inspection. Examine the structural-token alignments and three-dimensional superpositions of promising candidates.
  7. Refined ranking. Where more accurate coordinate-based ranking is required, repeat the search in RFoldSearch-TM mode.
  8. Extended search. Optionally, search the NuFold database to identify related structures among predicted RNA models.

Interpretation. Structural similarity may indicate a conserved RNA architecture even in the absence of detectable sequence similarity. However, structural similarity alone is insufficient to establish common ancestry or shared function, and inferences of this kind should be supported by independent evidence.

9. Programmatic access

10. Frequently asked questions

Can a search be performed using sequence information alone?

No. RFoldSearch requires a three-dimensional RNA structure as input. Where only a nucleotide sequence is available, a structural model must first be obtained by experimental determination or computational prediction.

Can RFoldSearch detect structural similarity between RNAs with low sequence identity?

Yes. Because RFoldSearch relies on learned representations of local geometry rather than primarily on nucleotide sequence, it can retrieve structurally similar RNAs whose sequences have substantially diverged.

Why may hits with identical sequence identity receive different structure scores?

Sequence identity does not imply conformational identity. Because the structure score reflects both alignment quality and geometric agreement, structures with similar or identical sequences but distinct conformations may receive different scores.

Why is the hit with the lowest RMSD not necessarily ranked first?

RMSD is computed only over aligned positions and does not account for alignment length or token-level alignment quality. A short alignment may yield a low RMSD without representing the strongest overall structural match.

Why are results not ordered by E-value?

Results are ranked by the structure score, which incorporates geometric agreement, whereas the E-value reflects only the statistical significance of the structural-token alignment score. The two orderings may therefore differ.

Which search mode is most appropriate?

The default RFoldSearch mode is recommended for routine and large-scale searches. RFoldSearch-TM is recommended when accurate coordinate-based structural ranking is the primary consideration.

Can predicted structures be used?

Yes. Predicted RNA structures may be submitted as queries, and the NuFold database provides a large collection of predicted structures as search targets. In both cases, the uncertainty inherent in structure prediction should be taken into account when interpreting results; pLDDT values, where available, indicate model confidence.

Does structural similarity imply homology?

Not necessarily. Structural similarity may reflect conserved architecture or a possible evolutionary relationship, but establishing homology requires additional sequence, functional or phylogenetic evidence.
